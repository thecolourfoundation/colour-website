Programme 1

Copper-diamond thermal composites

Diamond conducts heat far better than copper, and copper is easier to shape. A composite aims to combine both, with thermal expansion tuned by the diamond fraction. The interface between the two decides the result.

LabelStatementSource
EstablishedCopper does not wet diamond, so the interface limits heat transfer.Textbooks; interface reviews
EstablishedCarbide-forming elements (Ti, Cr, W, Mo, Zr, B) can bond the two, but a thick layer blocks heat.Reviews, 2020 and 2025
ReportedAbout 690 W/mK with an interface conductance of 45 MW/m2K, using W-coated 200 micron diamond at 1:1 volume.Abyzov et al., Materials and Design, 2015
Reported554 W/mK with only 93 MPa tensile strength in Ti-doped copper, showing the conductivity and strength trade-off.University of Waikato study
ReportedAbout 800 W/mK and low thermal expansion in a commercial composite.Element Six (manufacturer statement)
HypothesisA controlled interface and tuned expansion could reduce thermal strain in power-module baseplates.Colour. Untested.
UnknownMachining cost and behaviour at plate scale.No data found

Programme 2

High-temperature magnets with less heavy rare earth

Dysprosium and terbium keep sintered NdFeB magnets from weakening in hot motors, but they are scarce. Two techniques reduce the need: grain boundary diffusion and hydrogen processing of magnet scrap.

LabelStatementSource
EstablishedGrain boundary diffusion places heavy rare earths near grain surfaces, raising coercivity with a small loss of remanence.Reviews, 2021 and 2025
ReportedHydrogen processing can recover about 90% of magnetic properties under low temperature and pressure with rare earth hydride additions.Habibzadeh et al., ACS Omega, 2023
ReportedThe hydrogen scrap route is patented by the University of Birmingham, with HyProMag as exclusive licensee.HyProMag regulatory filing, 2024
HypothesisRecycled feed combined with diffusion could meet a high-temperature target with less heavy rare earth, repeatably.Colour. Untested.
UnknownFeedstock consistency across scrap sources, and freedom to operate.No data found
