Mission

Shorten the distance between a specification and a verified material, and publish what we learn.

Advanced hardware is limited by materials that are hard to specify, slow to develop and difficult to compare. Colour exists to make that path shorter and its evidence easier to trust.

01 · The challenge

Three problems under modern hardware.

01

Heat sets the limit

Thermal materials maker Element Six states that over half of electronic device failures are linked to heat. In power modules, a copper baseplate expands more than three times as much as the aluminium nitride substrate soldered to it (handbook values of about 16.5 and 4.5 ppm/K), which strains joints through every thermal cycle.

02

Supply is concentrated

The International Energy Agency reports that demand for magnet rare earths has doubled since 2015, and that China placed export controls on seven heavy rare earths in April 2025.

03

Evidence is hard to compare

Reported copper-diamond conductivities range from about 550 to 690 W/mK across studies that differ in diamond size, interface layer and method, and conductivity often trades against strength.

03 · How we work

One loop from specification to verified sample.

01

Specify

Customer needs become measurable fields (temperature, conductivity, expansion, size, tolerance), agreed in writing.

02

Design

Literature, simulation and recorded runs narrow the experiments worth running.

03

Make and measure

Samples are produced and tested with the same methods every time, each run recorded and traceable.

04

Verify

Independent third-party testing, then a datasheet and batch record. Any figure we have not measured is labelled a target.
